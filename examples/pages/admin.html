On 21 November 2026 we checked 60 user accounts and 7 sign-in policies in Microsoft 365 for security gaps and unused licences.

Security findings, most urgent first

  • Administrators without a second sign-in step (MFA): 2 enabled accounts (user-005, user-008). An administrator account that is protected only by a password is the easiest way into the company's mail and files.
  • Too many global administrators: 5 accounts hold the role, and the limit set for this check is 4. This is a setting of the tenant and names no person. Every extra global administrator is one more account that can change everything.
  • Old sign-in methods are not blocked. No switched-on policy blocks legacy authentication, the older ways of signing in that cannot ask for a second step, and the tenant's security defaults, which would block them, are off. One policy that would block them, policy-001, is still in report-only mode. This is a tenant setting and names no person.
  • Sign-in policies left in report-only mode for more than 30 days: 2 policies (policy-001, created 45 days ago, and policy-002, created 31 days ago). In report-only mode a policy only records what it would have done; it protects nothing. No person is named here.
  • Guest accounts with no sign-in for more than 90 days, or none on record: 2 accounts (user-001, user-004). Outside people who may no longer work with you can still reach company information.

Cost findings

  • Active accounts with a licence and no sign-in for more than 90 days: 7 accounts, 129.2 units a month.
  • Disabled accounts that still hold a licence: 2 accounts (user-010, user-012), 34.1 units a month.
  • A licensed account with no sign-in on record, created more than 30 days ago: 1 account (user-002), 6.1 units a month.

A last sign-in before 1 December 2023 is not on record, so an account with no sign-in on record may still have been used before then.

Total reclaimable cost: 169.4 units a month. These units were invented for this demo. They are not real prices, and no saving is promised.
